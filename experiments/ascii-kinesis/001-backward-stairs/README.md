# ASCII Kinesis 001 — Backward Stairs / Diagonal Hip Shift

## Observation

A user-supplied live movement clip showed backward descent of a short rail-less stair, a turn at the bottom, and backward ascent. During ascent, the performer discovered that a diagonal pelvis/hip shift made the movement substantially easier than attempting a naive straight-backward rise.

The source video remains a conversation upload and is not committed here. This directory persists the derived motion abstraction, structured motion data, compiled ASCII video, reconstruction source, and provenance boundary.

## What this entry proves

It proves that this specific specimen can be represented as literal ASCII source/contact poses and compiled into a coherent motion artifact while preserving the observed distinction between descent and ascent.

It does NOT prove that backward stair travel is generally safe, that this is the final movement form, or that the candidate Embodied Compression interpretation is universal.

## Tracked repository files

- `motion.json` — raw structured experiment and pose data.
- `motion.uapl` — UAPL v1 pose projection.
- `motion.uat` — UAT v1 timing opinion.
- `backward-stairs-ascii.mp4` — compiled ASCII presentation.
- `manifest.json` — anthology identity / compatibility / learning candidates.
- `build.py` — reconstruction source for this specimen.

## Generated delivery files

`build.py` deterministically emits:

- `contact-sheet.txt` — raw literal ASCII contact sheet;
- `backward-stairs.motion.html` — self-contained human delivery surface embedding the MP4, raw ASCII contact sheet, raw JSON, UAPL/UAT source, and manifest.

The generated HTML is the object delivered to the human. It is deliberately not required to be a second checked-in copy of embedded binary media; the repository persists the source, compiled media, and builder needed to reproduce it. Previously delivered self-contained HTML remains valid under the backward-compatibility law.
