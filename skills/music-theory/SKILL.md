# Music Theory

Status: SOURCE SKILL / NOT YET RUNTIME-INJECTED
Owner: Drew
Purpose: turn musical language, timing, arrangement, sound design, and scheduling metaphors into testable structure instead of decorative vocabulary.

## Trigger

Invoke when work depends materially on rhythm, meter, tempo, harmony, melody, motif, cadence, arrangement, tension/release, psychoacoustic masking, musical timing, or a metaphor such as **waltz** that is being used as an orchestration rule.

## Prime law

If a musical term controls behavior, resolve its technical meaning before using it as governance.

`MUSICAL WORD -> TECHNICAL PROPERTY -> SYSTEM ANALOG -> OBSERVABLE TEST`

A metaphor that produces no observable constraint is decoration, not machinery.

## Minimum vocabulary

- **Pulse / beat**: recurring temporal reference.
- **Tempo**: rate of pulse; never confuse rate with ordering.
- **Meter**: grouping of beats into recurring accent structure.
- **Waltz**: normally triple meter: one strong beat followed by two weaker beats (`ONE two three`). In Fortress orchestration this means ordered complementary phases with a returning downbeat, not three identical workers firing together.
- **Rhythm**: event durations/onsets against pulse and meter.
- **Syncopation**: meaningful displacement/emphasis away from expected strong positions; useful only when the baseline pulse is established.
- **Motif**: small recognizable unit that survives transformation.
- **Cadence**: closure/continuation signal; distinguish resolved stop from tension that demands continuation.
- **Harmony**: simultaneous relationships; in orchestration, independent lanes may coexist only when they do not destructively compete for the same authority/resource.
- **Counterpoint**: independent lines that remain individually coherent while interacting.
- **Dynamics**: controlled intensity over time; louder/more workers is not automatically escalation.
- **Tension/release**: accumulated expectation followed by meaningful resolution.
- **Negative space/rest**: intentional silence is structure, not missing output.

## Waltz gate

Before calling orchestration a waltz, inspect actual timestamps/receipts.

Required evidence:
1. identify the downbeat/material transition;
2. identify successor beat(s) that react to that transition rather than duplicate it;
3. preserve temporal ordering from receipts/timestamps;
4. show the return condition that starts the next measure;
5. reject simultaneous duplicate dispatch as `NOT_A_WALTZ`;
6. reject post-hoc narration of already-completed worker motion as `CONDUCTOR_LATE`.

Canonical orchestration analogy:

`1 WORK / MATERIAL CHANGE -> 2 EVIDENCE + JUDGMENT -> 3 HARVEST / JOY / SUCCESSOR -> next 1`

The exact owning workflow may refine these phases. Music Theory does not steal orchestration ownership.

## Audio application

For actual music/speech/sound work, reason explicitly about:
- BPM and perceived pulse;
- meter and phrase length;
- onset/timing and micro-rhythm;
- pitch vs formant;
- interval/chord function where tonal material exists;
- motif/repetition tolerance;
- spectral density and masking;
- dynamics/headroom;
- cadence and transition cues;
- silence/negative space;
- intended listening context and accessibility.

Prefer measurable/renderable mutations over prose theory. Change one bounded musical dimension when evaluating cause/effect.

## Anti-patterns

- saying `waltz` because three things exist;
- treating timestamps as metadata instead of rhythm evidence;
- equating escalation with volume, speed, or worker count;
- using theory vocabulary without changing a test or artifact;
- generic `music theory study` inside a campaign with no reusable skill;
- claiming musical quality from code/render success without listening/perceptual evidence.

## Receipt

A Music Theory invocation records: term/concept used, technical interpretation, evidence inspected, resulting constraint/mutation, and perceptual/runtime evidence when applicable.
