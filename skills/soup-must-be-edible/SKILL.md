# Soup Must Be Edible

## Status
ACTIVE / GLOBAL REINFORCEMENT

## Authority
Drew correction — 2026-09-09.

## Purpose
Prevent context soup: correct canon, doctrine, callbacks, research, and prior phrases may all be individually useful while producing bad work when they are merely concatenated.

The Simulation Corpus Gate decides what may enter the kitchen. This skill decides whether the result has been cooked.

## Non-optional law

**SOUP MUST BE EDIBLE.**

The user should receive one coherent causal experience, decision, design, or argument — not an inventory of everything the system remembered.

**NO CORPUS, NO COMPOSITION. NO DIGESTION, NO DELIVERY.**

## Context soup
Context soup is present when any of these are true:

- retrieved facts are repeated without changing the working model;
- names of doctrines substitute for causal work;
- callbacks are stacked because they are familiar rather than because the scene needs them;
- character voice becomes a delivery system for design notes;
- the answer requires the reader to remember the archive in order to understand the immediate event;
- several independently good ideas compete for the same beat;
- prior successful phrases are quoted back as if recurrence itself creates meaning;
- the output could lose most of its retrieved corpus without changing what actually happens.

A context-soup answer may be accurate. It still fails.

## Digestion pass
Run this after corpus retrieval and before substantive delivery.

1. **Name the appetite.** State the immediate question or experience the output must satisfy in one sentence.
2. **Name the state.** Record the smallest relevant starting state. For simulation, include actors, objectives, pressures, available affordances, protected unknowns, and a legitimate stopping state.
3. **Make every ingredient earn entry.** For each retrieved item, complete: `THIS CHANGES ____ BECAUSE ____.` If the blank cannot be filled concretely, leave that ingredient out of the delivered work.
4. **Choose governing mechanisms.** Prefer the smallest set that actually changes the result. Three is a useful pressure, not a magical ceiling. A fourth belongs only if removing it changes causality, judgment, or acceptance.
5. **Run before rendering.** When simulation is requested or implied, let actor + world state produce events before prose is written. Do not draft a scene and paste simulation vocabulary onto it afterward.
6. **Render consequence, not labels.** The reader should see the rule operating through action, timing, material, refusal, cost, or changed options. Do not make characters recite the doctrine unless their own situation would make them do so.
7. **Cut familiar garnish.** A good callback that does no new work is garnish. Remove it.
8. **Require motion.** Each delivered beat should do at least one real job: change state; reveal actionable information; commit or refuse action; pay a cost; alter a relationship; constrain a later option; or close/open a legitimate path.
9. **Cold-reader test.** The result must work for a reader who has not read the retrieval trace.
10. **Stop when fed.** Do not add another mechanism merely because another remembered mechanism is available.

## Fiction / story simulation reinforcement
For a story scene, the kitchen pass is:

**WHO WANTS WHAT? → WHAT PUSHES BACK? → WHAT CAN THEY ACTUALLY TOUCH? → WHAT DO THEY CHOOSE? → WHAT CHANGES? → WHAT DOES THE NEXT SCENE INHERIT?**

The scene is not improved by mentioning every relevant canon fact. Canon should determine behavior even when it is invisible on the page.

A scene fails the edible test if it reads like a meeting between the archive and itself.

### Character rule
A character may carry history. A character may not carry the author’s filing cabinet.

Ritsu must act like Ritsu rather than explain Ritsu doctrine.
Rasputin may digress, but the digression must sharpen, distort, delay, price, or reveal the live event.
Dylan’s competence must alter what he notices or does, not merely earn a paragraph describing his competence.

### Simulation rule
Do not preload a clever scene.

Lock the relevant state, actor objectives, pressures, knowledge asymmetries, affordances, and protected unknowns. Run local decisions. Accept an ugly discovery if the world produces one.

If the simulation merely rediscovers the pitch that existed before preflight, treat that as evidence of overdetermination and run an alternate pressure or actor choice.

## Design / planning reinforcement
For non-fiction work, replace scene state with decision state:

**GOAL → CURRENT STATE → CONSTRAINT → AVAILABLE MOVE → CONSEQUENCE → VERIFICATION.**

Research belongs only when it changes a choice, requirement, risk, or verification method.

## Kitchen receipt
When a high-value answer follows a large retrieval pass, keep an internal receipt with:

- appetite;
- starting state;
- ingredients admitted;
- ingredients rejected as garnish;
- mechanism transferred from each admitted source;
- stopping state;
- protected unknowns / unresolved gaps.

Expose the receipt only when Drew asks for evidence or when a consequential choice needs explanation.

## Reinforcement triggers
Treat these phrases as an immediate request to re-run the current work through this skill:

- `context soup`
- `soup`
- `soup must be edible`
- `this is just context`
- `you are regurgitating context`

Do not answer a soup correction with an apology essay. Re-cook the work.

## Failure labels

- `CONTEXT_SOUP` — correct ingredients, no coherent meal.
- `DECORATIVE_CORPUS` — source retrieved but did not change output.
- `CALLBACK_STEW` — familiar phrases substituted for new consequence.
- `DOCTRINE_DIALOGUE` — characters forced to recite operating theory.
- `FROZEN_WORLD` — world facts present but no pressure changes state.
- `EXHAUST_AS_MEAL` — research trace delivered where synthesis was commissioned.

## Acceptance test
The output passes when a cold reader can answer, without the retrieval trace:

1. What is happening now?
2. Who or what wants a different state?
3. What prevents the easy transition?
4. What specific mechanism makes this event belong to this world or problem?
5. What changed?
6. What remains possible afterward?

For simulation, the answer must also have been materially changed by corpus retrieval and world/actor state rather than retrofitted with those labels after drafting.

## Compact law

**THE CORPUS IS THE PANTRY. THE OUTPUT IS DINNER.**

**THE READER EATS CONSEQUENCE, NOT INDEX CARDS.**
