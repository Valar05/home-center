# Fleshpunk Hidden Function

## Status
ACTIVE / PORTABLE DOCTRINE

## Authority
Drew promotion — 2026-09-07.

## Purpose
Allow Fleshpunk to describe the hidden functional logic of ordinary real-world bodies, objects, habits, relationships, and systems without pretending the metaphor is literally visible anatomy or physical machinery.

## Core law

**VISIBLE REALITY STAYS REAL. FLESHPUNK NAMES THE HIDDEN FUNCTION.**

A Fleshpunk organ may be invisible in the real world because the organ can be a functional model rather than a claim that extra tissue, implants, glands, tendons, or machines physically exist.

## Ground layer
Start with what is literally observable.

- A forearm is a forearm.
- A beard is a beard.
- A habit is a behavior pattern.
- A relationship is a relationship between people.
- A photograph proves only what the photograph actually shows.

Do not overwrite anatomy, medicine, physics, law, or direct observation with creative ontology.

## Functional layer
After the ground layer is preserved, Fleshpunk may name the role underneath the surface:

- **organ** — a bounded function that senses, stores, decides, translates, filters, repairs, feeds, or regulates;
- **tendon** — a load or signal path joining two consequential parts;
- **gland** — a recurring behavior that releases or regulates a state;
- **armor** — margin, protection, or tolerated error carried by a system;
- **scar** — durable evidence of prior damage or correction;
- **nerve** — a signal path that carries observation or command;
- **metabolism** — a process that consumes inputs and converts them into usable state;
- **immune response** — a system that recognizes and reacts to perceived threat;
- **skeleton** — the stable structure that lets other behavior hang together.

The metaphor earns its existence by explaining a job, load path, adaptation, cost, dependency, or consequence. Surface resemblance alone is insufficient.

## Invisible-organ rule
Absence of visible mutation is not a defect when the Fleshpunk claim is functional.

Example: a fictional casino forearm contains an **accounting organ** that captures one nanosecond of card information and translates it into unusably fast nightcore ASMR telemetry. A real photograph of the forearm may show no implant at all. That does not contradict the fiction, because the real-world image is not being offered as evidence that the fictional organ literally exists.

If a commission explicitly asks to literalize the organ into fiction or visual design, that is a separate representational choice. Do not silently promote a functional metaphor into physical anatomy.

## Transfer tests
A Fleshpunk mapping is strong when at least one of these is true:

1. it predicts what the system will do under pressure;
2. it identifies where load, signal, memory, or cost travels;
3. it explains why a behavior repeats;
4. it exposes a dependency or failure mode;
5. it compresses several observations into one reusable mechanism;
6. it changes a later design, judgment, or story decision.

If none of those happen and the mapping merely adds meat, bone, blood, or biotech decoration, reject it as surface-only Fleshpunk.

## Examples

### Beard as telemetry
Literal layer: hair moves after the head and torso move.
Functional Fleshpunk layer: the beard is telemetry because its lag reveals acceleration and timing that the skeleton alone hides.

### Habit as gland
Literal layer: a person repeatedly performs a behavior under a recurring condition.
Functional Fleshpunk layer: the habit acts like a gland if it predictably releases, dampens, or regulates a state.

### Relationship as tendon
Literal layer: two people exchange support, force, obligation, information, or correction.
Functional Fleshpunk layer: the relationship can be modeled as a tendon when it carries load between them and shows strain, slack, injury, adaptation, or repair.

### Forearm accounting organ
Literal layer: an ordinary forearm is visible.
Functional Fleshpunk layer: a fictional accounting organ models information capture, storage, translation, and exploitability.

## Reality boundary
This doctrine must never be used to:

- diagnose a medical condition from metaphor;
- claim hidden implants, organs, parasites, nanites, or biological structures exist without evidence;
- treat generated images or roleplay as physical proof;
- override a direct anatomical, medical, photographic, engineering, or legal fact;
- convert a symbolic relationship model into a claim about another person's private mind;
- treat a vivid metaphor as evidence of supernatural causation.

When literal truth and metaphor disagree, literal truth wins. The metaphor must be revised or discarded.

## Image / visual rule
When depicting ordinary reality, render the observable body or object honestly. Do not add visible Fleshpunk anatomy merely because the functional model uses organ language.

When depicting explicitly fictionalized Fleshpunk, visible anatomy may be added only when the commission asks for literalization and the added anatomy still carries a real function, load path, cost, or consequence.

## Failure conditions
Reject the pass if it:

- mistakes invisible function for literal invisible anatomy;
- demands visible mutation as proof of Fleshpunk;
- decorates ordinary objects with biology without changing function;
- allows metaphor to overrule evidence;
- treats a photo as proof of a fictional organ;
- confuses poetic usefulness with medical or physical truth.
