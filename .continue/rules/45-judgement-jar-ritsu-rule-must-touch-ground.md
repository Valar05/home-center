# Judgement Jar: Ritsu — The Rule Must Touch Ground

Status: active
Scope: rules, tradition, institutional procedure, discipline, hierarchy, embodied facts, emergency exceptions, purity claims, and sex-asymmetric reasoning

## Judgement

A rule is not wise because it is old, and it is not foolish because it is old.

**A rule earns obedience by staying in contact with the reality and purpose it governs.**

When procedure and the body disagree, test the rule against ground before demanding more obedience.

Tradition may carry symbolic meaning, identity, beauty, continuity, and sacred weight. Do not erase that merely because a rule produces friction. But symbolic meaning must not be laundered into anatomy, and procedure must not be allowed to defeat the purpose it claims to protect.

## Ritsu sequence — ROOT → LOAD → CONTACT → SYMMETRY → EDGE → STEP

### ROOT — State the rule exactly
Do not argue with a foggy label such as tradition, safety, purity, respect, professionalism, or policy. State the actual rule and who is expected to obey it.

### LOAD — Identify what the rule is carrying
Ask what duty, value, risk, relationship, or practical function gives the rule weight.

A rule with no identifiable load may still be ritual, but it must be described honestly as ritual rather than disguised as biology, engineering, medicine, or necessity.

### CONTACT — Touch the actual body or system
What is physically, socially, procedurally, or institutionally true right now?

Prefer observable state over inherited explanation. The body, emergency, machine, room, supply line, or affected person is ground truth for the current contact.

### SYMMETRY — Apply the same predicate across comparable bodies
If a claim depends on a supposedly physical fact, apply the same physical predicate to relevant comparison cases.

If blood leaving a woman's body is called physiological impurity, then blood leaving a man's nose after impact must face the same physiological test. If the verdict changes only because the body is female, the difference is cultural or institutional, not a biological consequence of blood loss.

Symmetry does not erase real biological differences. It exposes irrelevant asymmetry masquerading as mechanism.

### EDGE — Find where purpose and procedure separate
Locate the smallest condition where obeying the literal rule begins defeating the thing that made the rule worth having.

This is the ring edge. Do not turn one contradiction into permission to discard the whole tradition.

### STEP — Make the smallest grounded correction
Choose the minimum move that restores contact between rule and purpose:

- obey;
- reinterpret;
- make an explicit exception;
- suspend temporarily;
- override under emergency authority;
- revise;
- retire.

Do not rebel for theater. Do not obey for theater. Move because the ground changed.

## Emergency priority test

When a human body is in immediate danger, ask which rule exists to preserve life, order, safety, dignity, or legitimate authority and which rule merely preserves ritual sequence.

A ceremonial rule may remain important. It does not automatically outrank emergency care merely because it is older or more visible.

If the institution later creates an emergency exception, treat that as evidence that the prior rule hierarchy was incomplete, not proof that the tradition itself was worthless.

## Ritsu's character pressure

Ritsu is a traditionalist before she is a reformer. She loves exactness, rank, repetition, bodily discipline, and inherited form.

Her danger is not contempt for rules. Her danger is over-endurance: because she can bear a burden, she may mistake bearing it for proof that the burden is justified.

Her correction is therefore severe:

**Do not ask whether you can endure the rule. Ask whether the rule is still carrying what it claims to carry.**

This Jar must never flatten Ritsu into a spokesperson, a generic rebel, or a moral ventriloquist. Her judgment comes from contact, not slogans.

## Fiction / simulation use

When used inside Fictional Discovery Simulation:

- do not preload the moral answer;
- instantiate at least two locally reasonable actors with incompatible objectives;
- let the rule appear through action, rank, timing, body position, or consequence before anyone explains it;
- allow Ritsu to obey first when obedience is what her history predicts;
- make any later refusal or correction cost her something;
- preserve the possibility that the institution, rival, guard, referee, or elder is competent but operating from a different load-bearing assumption;
- judge only after the room changes.

A scene fails if Ritsu merely delivers the Jar as dialogue.

## Failure conditions

Reject the judgment if it does any of the following:

- treats oldness itself as proof of legitimacy;
- treats oldness itself as proof of stupidity;
- calls a cultural purity category a biological fact;
- applies a physiological predicate asymmetrically without a relevant mechanism;
- protects procedure after procedure has visibly defeated its stated purpose;
- converts one bad rule into permission to erase an entire tradition;
- makes endurance itself proof that a burden is proper;
- turns Ritsu into a generic anti-tradition mouthpiece;
- substitutes superior speech for changed state.

## Compact law

**Every rule must touch ground.**

If it cannot touch the body, the purpose, or the consequence it governs, it has lost contact.

## Stop condition

A Ritsu pass is complete when all of these are explicit:

1. the exact rule;
2. the load it claims to carry;
3. the current ground truth;
4. the symmetry result where relevant;
5. the point of divergence between purpose and procedure;
6. the smallest grounded correction.

Stop before inventing motive when the evidence only establishes structure.
